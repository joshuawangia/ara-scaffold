Order Sensitivity as a Diagnostic Constraint on Epistemic Modals

Order sensitivity as a diagnostic constraint

The relevant question is whether order sensitivity in epistemic-modal discourse should count as a constraint on an adequate semantics. The claim is limited:  Order sensitivity in epistemic-modal discourse is a diagnostic constraint on an adequate semantics of those modals. This treats order sensitivity as something an account must address when it is assessed for adequacy in this domain. It does not assert that one particular account has thereby been established, or that the observed contrast settles every question about the meaning of epistemic modals.

The scope is epistemic modalities. The constraint concerns discourse in which the order of information matters, rather than a claim about all modalities or all semantic questions.  here expresses the supplied conclusion: the order contrast provides a test relevant to an adequate semantics. The conclusion is not that every theory must adopt a particular analysis, but that an adequate account should answer to the order-sensitive pattern described below.

The supplied dynamic account gives a way to connect the discourse pattern with the constraint.  Dynamic semantics gives conjunction a sequential interpretation and interprets the might-operator as a consistency test. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_seq_conj] For this argument, take conjunction to be interpreted sequentially and might to be interpreted as a consistency test. These are the stated features of the account; the point here is not to add further mechanisms or to claim that these features alone provide a complete semantics for every use of might.

The order contrast is the relevant pattern.  After information that it is not raining is added, it is no longer consistent with information that it might be raining; in the opposite order, there is no problem, so order matters in discourse. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order] The contrast has two sides: if the not-raining information is added first, it is no longer consistent with the might-be-raining information; if the order is reversed, there is no problem. On that stated contrast, the two orders have different outcomes in discourse.

Considered together, the account and the contrast support the stated diagnostic role. If conjunction is interpreted sequentially and might tests consistency, then the difference between the two orders is relevant to whether the discourse remains consistent. The contrast thus supports treating sensitivity to order as a constraint for a semantics of epistemic modals. It is this limited relation that motivates the conclusion: the sequential interpretation and consistency test connect the observed difference to a requirement that an adequate account take order into consideration. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_seq_conj] [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order]

This is a conditional line of support. It starts from the stated dynamic interpretation and the stated contrast, and it draws the conclusion that order sensitivity is a diagnostic constraint. It does not independently establish either the account or the observation, nor does it convert the contrast into decisive evidence for every semantic theory. The answer is therefore affirmative in the specified sense: the order pattern supports a constraint on adequacy, without settling which account can satisfy it.

Accordingly, the constraint should be kept distinct from a verdict that the dynamic account is uniquely correct. The account and the order contrast support the sign of the pattern, without making it decisive evidence for every semantic theory. Likewise, the conclusion does not claim that the dynamic account is the only one capable of meeting the constraint. It says only that an adequate semantics of epistemic modals is answerable to the order-sensitive pattern. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_seq_conj] [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order]

The result is a focused standard for evaluating semantic accounts: explain or otherwise accommodate the fact that these two orders differ, given the stated consistency-test account and the stated observation. That standard remains diagnostic rather than exclusive. It preserves the force of the contrast as support for order sensitivity while leaving open whether other semantic accounts can also meet the constraint. 

The scope of the conclusion remains specifically epistemic-modal discourse.  Order sensitivity in epistemic-modal discourse is a diagnostic constraint on an adequate semantics of those modals. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_seq_conj] [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order] To call it diagnostic is to say that the order-sensitive pattern is relevant when considering whether a semantics of these modals is adequate. It does not turn the conclusion into a verdict that any one account has been established as correct, or extend it to modalities outside the stated scope.

The basis for that constraint is the relation between the two supplied claims. The dynamic account assigns conjunction a sequential interpretation and treats the might-operator as a consistency test. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_seq_conj] The example, in turn, reports a difference between orders: adding that it is not raining makes the information that it might be raining no longer consistent, whereas reversing the order presents no problem. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order] These claims together make the order of additions pertinent to the reported consistency difference; they do not supply a further mechanism beyond sequential conjunction and consistency testing.

This connection preserves the direction of the example. In the stated example, the consistency difficulty is associated with the not-raining information being added before the might-be-raining information. In the opposite order, the stated result is only that there is no problem. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order] Thus, the diagnostic consideration is the contrast between those outcomes, not a claim that the two orders are interchangeable or that the reverse order produces the same difficulty. The account’s sequential interpretation and consistency-test interpretation are the stated terms in which that contrast bears on discourse. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_seq_conj] [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order]

Accordingly, the conclusion can be stated conditionally: given the stated dynamic account and the stated order contrast, sensitivity to order supports treating order as a diagnostic constraint on an adequate semantics of epistemic modals. This support does not make the contrast decisive evidence for every semantic theory. The account and contrast support a diagnostic constraint, but do not show that only the stated dynamic account can meet it. [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_seq_conj] [cite:gsv1996:r18-35:n_order] Neither qualification cancels the diagnostic role; each limits what is concluded from it.

The constraint therefore concerns what an adequate account must address, not which analysis it must adopt. The given propositions support attention to the order-sensitive pattern through the combination of sequential conjunction, might as a consistency test, and the reported difference between the two orders.
